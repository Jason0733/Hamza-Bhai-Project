coastal poetry & stories

Under the Palms is a collection of coastal poems shaped by sunlight, saltwater, and the quiet moments that stay with us long after the tide goes out.

Rooted in the Gulf Coast and carried by memory, these pieces move through calm mornings, shifting seasons, familiar shorelines, and the everyday resilience that comes from choosing peace in a world that doesn’t always offer it. Through poems, stories, and a final family section, Kelley explores the places that steady us, the journeys that change us, and the humor that keeps us afloat.

from Under the Palms

Saltwater

When the world spins loud and the headlines feel heavier than the sky above them, I carry my chaos to the shoreline. The salt air meets me first a soft sting, a familiar kiss, as if the ocean has been waiting to rinse the noise from my mind. I unroll my mat on sand still warm from the sun's last blessing, a small declaration that peace is still possible, even here, even now. The tide breathes for me until I remember how to breathe for myself. Chaos never really leaves. It just softens under saltwater light. And when I rise, the ocean rises with me steady, familiar, reminding me that calm is not the absence of chaos, but the courage to breathe through it.

Poem 02

Under the Palms

Under the palms is heaven on earth to me. It's where my head clears, where my shoulders drop, where the sun remembers how to be gentle. It's the place where my best thinking happens not in meetings, not in traffic, but right here, with the breeze doing all the heavy lifting. Under the palms, I remember who I am before the world gets loud again.
Poem 03

Protecting my Peace

Kindness is my strength not a softness that breaks, but a softness that holds. It's the steady breath I return to when old expectations try to pull me back into storms I've already outgrown. It's the quiet power that lets me stand firm without raising my voice, and walk away without carrying guilt that was never mine.
Poem 04

Sand, Seashells
& Sunsets

Give me sand to walk on, the kind that remembers every step I've taken and still lets me start fresh with the next one. Give me seashells to gather, little reminders that even broken things can be beautiful when the tide has had time to soften them. Give me sunsets to chase, slow and steady, the sky teaching me that endings can be gentle and still breathtaking. Give me days like this simple, quiet, full of small treasures that don't ask for anything except that I notice them.
— pocket collection, sunday
Poem 05 · A benediction

Peace

Required daily, like tide and moon, a rhythm I return to. I open my heart the way the shoreline opens to morning light slowly, softly, without apology. And in that quiet, stillness finds me.

Author's note

Softening
with time.

This book began in a way I never expected. My fifteen-year-old granddaughter had been writing a novel, a good one, until her spark dimmed and the pages went still. One Saturday morning, we stopped for coffee and wandered into a women's poetry reading. We didn't plan it. We simply walked in and felt something shift.

A woman stood and read an abecedarian. I had never heard of one. It was beautiful and brave, and it nudged something awake in me. I went home and wrote one. Then another. Then poems about everyday life — the past, the present, the future — and somewhere in the middle of my writing, something stirred in her too. She picked up her book again. She is writing as I write.

This collection grew from that shared spark — a circle of inspiration that passed from her to me, and back to her again.

Kelley
